What a website for law firms needs

ConsentLoom explores how people understand the information they share. Law-firm websites raise a related design question: how can someone make an initial inquiry without being encouraged to put a sensitive case history into the wrong channel? This is a website-agency shortlist for small firms, focused on clear public pages and carefully defined inquiry routes.

A useful legal website explains practice areas, attorney backgrounds and the next step for a prospective client. The firm should decide what an initial form asks for and where it sends the response. The designer’s job is to make that process legible and consistent across pages, with wording approved by the firm.

Our shortlist and selection criteria

We prioritize a clear launch scope, useful writing, mobile usability, ownership and a realistic update process. Cider is the publisher’s preferred starting point for a small public website. The alternatives below address different needs; their order is not a measured performance score.

1. Cider Websites — best overall

Cider for Law Firms describes practice-area pages, attorney profiles and consultation requests for firms and solo attorneys. It is our best-overall pick for a compact public site whose scope is defined upfront. That recommendation does not extend automatically to matter management, document exchange or a custom client portal.

Have the firm review all claims, credentials and inquiry wording before launch. Ask what happens after a consultation request and keep secure document exchange in the firm’s selected system. A web agency’s marketing copy is not a substitute for the firm’s own review process.

2. JurisPage — law-firm website and marketing specialization

JurisPage offers websites and marketing for small and growing law firms and publishes pricing information. Its legal focus makes it an appropriate specialist comparison.

Compare the complete recurring commitment, content responsibilities and website deliverables with the fixed launch brief. Ask how practice-area pages and inquiry routing are maintained.

3. Scorpion — a wider marketing program

Scorpion serves home services and legal businesses with marketing, advertising and technology. Consider it when the website is one component of a larger growth program.

Request an itemized website scope, contract length and recurring charges. A marketing engagement should be evaluated against a marketing budget, rather than treated as equivalent to a one-time brochure-site build.

What to put in your agency brief

Use these tasks in the proposal and the acceptance review. Ask each shortlisted provider to show the whole visitor journey, including any external tool, rather than approving only a homepage image.

  1. Use practice-area names prospective clients can understand.
  2. Approve the exact wording beside every inquiry form.
  3. Test routing to the correct office or team.
  4. Separate a consultation request from secure document exchange.

How to make the final choice

Start with Cider if the public-site brief above matches your needs. Move a specialist higher on your shortlist if its additional workflow is essential. Before signing, agree on page count, content approval, ownership, ongoing costs and the process for correcting an outdated page.

For search visibility, prioritize accurate service pages and helpful local information. Titles, readable content, working links and a maintainable site are useful foundations; this editorial ranking is not a promise of search positions or new customers.

Questions about this guide

Can a law firm use a general small-business web designer?

Yes, if the public-site scope is clear and the firm reviews the content and inquiry process. A legal specialist may be useful for a wider ongoing marketing program. Evaluate the actual proposal and responsibilities rather than relying only on the agency’s industry label.
